                                                       Abstract. We initiate the study of sheaves on C ech closure spaces, providing a new, unified
                                                       approach to sheaf theory on many of the major classes of spaces of interest to applications:
                                                       topological spaces, finite simplicial complexes (seen as T0 topological spaces), graphs and digraphs
                                                       (both seen as closure spaces), and semi-pseudometric spaces, in particular those constructed
                                                       from a pseudometric spaces decorated with a privileged scale, which are those typically used for
                                                       topological data analysis. Our construction proceeds by constructing a Grothendieck topology
                                                       on the category M(cX ) of finite intersections of subsets of (X, cX ) with non-empty interior,
                                                       which is the natural generalization to closure spaces of the category O(X,  ) of open sets in a
                                                       topological space. We continue by constructing the sheaf and C ech cohomologies on M(cX ), and
                                                       standard constructions then give a spectral sequence between the C ech cohomology and the sheaf
                                                       cohomology on a site (M(cX ), J) which we use to identify several examples of non-topological
                                                       closure spaces with non-trivial sheaf cohomology.

                                                                                              1. Introduction

                                             Starting from its origins in algebraic topology, sheaf theory has become an indispensable part
                                          of homological algebra, and it has many important applications in fields as diverse as algebraic
                                          geometry and partial differential equations. In the past decade, particularly with the rise of topo-
                                          logical data analysis, there has been increased interest in extending the reach of sheaf theory to
                                          scientific and engineering applications, and a number of intriguing efforts have been made in that
                                          direction [28, 14, 10, 8, 4, 24, 26, 25]. The most common construction of this kind in the literature
                                          involves studying sheaves on a combinatorially defined space which may be given a topology, such
                                          as a simplicial complex with a T0 topology, either in the version on cell complexes first developed
                                          in [27] and revived in [9], where the T0 topology need not be used explicitly to develop much of the
                                          theory, or else on spaces of posets as in [26], where the T0 topology takes on a more central role.
                                          Related constructions of sheaf theory on simplicial complexes can also be found in Section 8.1 of
                                          [18].

                                             In this article, we introduce a new approach to the construction of sheaf theory on discrete and
                                          combinatorial spaces by constructing sheaves on C ech closure spaces, a category which contains
                                          many of the major classes of spaces of interest to applications: topological spaces, including finite
                                          simplicial complexes with a T0 topology on the collection of simplices, graphs and digraphs (where

   The algebraic topology of C ech closure spaces appears to have been first studied in [11, 12],
where it was used to construct new invariants of graphs and digraphs. After a long period of
inactivity, this idea was taken up again in [22, 23], where the development of homotopy theory on
C ech closure spaces was continued, and where it was shown that this framework also covers the
case of metric spaces which are endowed with a preferred scale, the scale indicating the minimal
radius of a metric ball around a point which must be contained in any neighborhood of that point.
Further work on the algebraic topology of closure spaces led to a unified view of a number of
discrete homotopy theories in [6], where it was shown that many of the discrete homotopy theories
on studied on graphs [3, 2, 16] can be expressed as a homotopy theory on C ech closure spaces
simply by changing the cylinder functor and the product used to define homotopy. Although not
mentioned in [6], both digital homotopy [1, 5] and the discrete homotopies studied in [21] may also
be expressed in terms of homotopies on appropriate closure spaces as well. Finally, it was also shown
in [6] that stability theorems for persistent versions of a functor (persistent homology, persistent
cohomology, persistent homotopy, etc) follow directly from the closure space homotopy invariance
of that functor, demonstrating the importance of the algebraic topology of closure spaces to more
traditional approaches to applied topology. Finally, the category of pseudotopological spaces, the
Cartesian closed hull of both the category of topological spaces and C ech closure spaces, was shown
to admit a Quillen-type model structure in [23], and this model structure was further shown in [13]
to be Quillen equivalent to the Quillen model structure on topological spaces.

   There are a number of obstacles which must be overcome in order to develop sheaf theory on
general C ech closure spaces. First, in most interesting closure spaces, there are simply not enough
open sets for the classical, topological sheaf cohomology to provide much information. We see this
already with the closure space (V, cG) induced by a connected graph G = (V, E). In this case,
the open sets of (V, cG) are simply the indiscrete topology on the vertex set, i.e. the topology
consisting only of {, V }, and, like the topology, the resulting sheaf theory is trivial. In closure
spaces, however, the interior of a set may be non-open, and there are many more non-open sets
with non-trivial (and non-open) interiors than there are open sets. Using these, we may form so-
called interior covers, which provides an apparent solution to the problem of the lack of open sets.
However, this leads to a second issue, which is that the category N (cX ) of subsets of (X, cX ) with
non-empty interior is not closed under finite intersections. Naively, one may try to address the issue
by simply adding the missing sets to N (cX ), and so we might posit that the category in which we
should work is not N (cX ), but rather the category which contains N (cX ) together with all of the
finite intersections of its elements, which we denote by M(cX ). However, while the sets in M(cX )
now form a topology on X, this topology is often too fine to be interesting. That is, if we build
sheaves using open covers of X in the topology given by M(cX ), then much of the structure we
wish to capture may be lost. We instead consider a kind of hybrid of these two options. We begin
with presheaves defined on the category whose objects are the subspaces of (X, cX ) induced by
     SHEAF THEORY FOR C ECH CLOSURE SPACES  3

inclusions of the sets M(cX ) into (X, cX ), and whose morphisms are inclusions of subspaces. (We
also call this category M(cX ) in a slight abuse of notation.) We then use interior covers of the
subspaces in M(cX ) to form a basis for the Grothendieck topology, and the sets in M(cX ) with
empty interior are used for the matching conditions in the sheafification process.

   After constructing the Grothendieck topology on M(cX ), we construct C ech cohomology on
closure spaces with coefficients in a presheaf, which is defined with respect to interior covers,
as it was in [20] for constant sheaves. We then recall the spectral sequence passing from C ech

cohomology to sheaf cohomology on a Grothendieck topology, and we use this spectral sequence to

produce examples of non-topological closure spaces, in particular closure spaces induced by graphs

and metric spaces with a privileged scale, which have non-trivial sheaf cohomology.

   Finally, the following examples use the above corollaries to produce non-topological closure spaces

Example 4.9. Consider the closure space (X, cG) generated by the cyclic graph G = (V, E), where
X = V = {0, . . . , n} and E consists of all pairs of the form (k, k + 1) or (k, k - 1), where all entries
are taken mod(n + 1). We see that U = {(k - 1, k, k + 1) | k = 0, . . . , n}, forms an interior coverof
(X, cG), and it is the maximal interior cover with respect to the partial order given by refinement. It
follows that H p(X, F ) = Hp(U , F ). When F is the constant sheaf F = Z, the latter is the simplicial
cohomology of the nerve of the cover U , and therefore the sheaf cohomology Hp(X, Z) = Hp(S1, Z),
for p = 0, 1 by Corollary 4.7.
